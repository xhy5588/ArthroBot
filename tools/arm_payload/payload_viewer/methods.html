The calculation

Reach is geometry. Lifting is a torque budget.

For each arm posture, we calculate what gravity asks of all six joints, then find the payload that fits inside every motor limit.

01

Find each weight’s lever arm

τj = uj · Σ [(ci − pj) × mig]

p is the joint pivot, u its rotation axis, and c a downstream part’s center of mass. Gravity is g = [0, 0, −9.81] m/s². The cross product gives the turning effect; the dot product keeps the part acting around that motor’s axis.

Why it matters: distance from the base alone is not a joint’s lever arm. Changing the posture or gripper orientation changes the torque.

02

Include the arm before the payload

τj(m) = aj + m kj

a is the torque from the arm, motors and gripper. k is the torque added by 1 kg at the grasp point, in N·m/kg. Payload mass m is the only value changing in this equation; the posture stays fixed.

Why it matters: the motor already spends some of its capacity holding the arm itself. Our model includes 3.297 kg of moving arm mass; the fixed base does not load the moving joints.

03

Respect every motor’s limit

|aj + m kj| ≤ Tj,   m ≥ 0

We solve each joint’s allowed mass interval and intersect all six intervals. The upper end of the intersection is the static payload ceiling for that posture. Here, each motor cap is 13 N·m.

Why keep the signs: the arm and payload can turn a joint in opposite directions. Subtracting their absolute torques would give the wrong answer in those postures.

What a colored point means

The original map groups sampled grasp positions into 2.5 cm cells and keeps the best payload posture found in each cell. A 2 kg point means at least one sampled posture in that cell can hold 2 kg in the static motor model.

“Any orientation” allows the sampler to choose a favorable gripper orientation. It does not mean every required module orientation works there. The downward mode checks only its stated approach-angle cone. Neither mode proves a continuous collision-free path.

What changing a rod changes

The preview recalculates joint positions, arm centers of mass, and gravity torque. It assumes the same tube material and cross section, with 8 g per 150 mm. It uses 262,144 poses and 5 cm cells, so it is a coarser comparison than the original map.

Geometric reach comes from the joint chain; payload filters the postures that can hold a load. For the current 150 + 150 mm rods, the joint-1-to-grasp chain sums to 0.722 m. That is an upper distance bound, not an attainable spherical workspace. An empty sampled cell is not proof of unreachable space.

dgrasp ≤ Σ ‖joint offset‖ + ‖tool offset‖

Why a lighter load cannot unlock the whole 1 m cube: reaching all corners of any 1 m cube requires a distance of at least √3 / 2 ≈ 0.866 m from a fixed pivot, even if it were placed at the cube’s center. The original arm’s 0.722 m bound is smaller. Mounting beside the cube requires still more reach.

What “static” leaves out

τmotor = M(q)q̈ + C(q, q̇)q̇ + g(q)

This rigid-body equation includes acceleration and velocity effects. At rest, g(q) is the motor’s gravity-compensation torque: the opposite sign to the external gravity moments in the table. Friction and contact forces require additional terms.

Grip slip, printed-part strength, flex, thermal duty and the 50 cm module’s clearance are not validated here. The original map uses an approximate self-collision screen; changed-length previews do not. Review the model assumptions.
